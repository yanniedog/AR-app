# History derivation and missing observations

Product history records `section-best-and-ribbon-normalization-v1`, the local representative-rate and ribbon transformation identity. This is not financial-ledger execution. A changed or missing derivation identity rebuilds source-bound cells while preserving revision rollback barriers.

Selected dates remain on the axis. A successfully verified complete catalogue can establish an absent product as null without requiring repeated core downloads. An unavailable dated source remains null/unverified and is retried on a later synchronization. Lines and bands break at these gaps; exact daily moves require adjacent observed cells. Separately labelled first-observed interval summaries retain their existing meaning.

Revised dates bind their immutable publication identities. Legacy dates refresh a bounded manifest selection, then bind deterministic parsed-manifest content plus the verified core digest. This is content integrity, not a signature or an assertion about raw manifest bytes. A failed refresh cannot certify a previous selection as current.

Producer compact histories use the separate `compact-ribbon-normalization-v1` identity. Their cached local normalization is reusable only with matching current source hashes and normalization version; they are not described as locally derived daily-core histories.

Aggregate and product synchronization reject superseded editions. Cache/state adoption checks the captured selection after asynchronous work; aggregate cache writes share the serialized core commit path. No history retry can lower a retained revision high-water mark.
